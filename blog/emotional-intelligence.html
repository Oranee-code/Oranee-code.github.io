Emotional Intelligence

What is Emotional Intelligence (EQ)?

Being smart with your emotions.

Emotional Intelligence is the ability to understand, manage, and use emotions—both your own and others’—in a positive way.
It’s about being aware of how you feel, why you feel that way, and how your feelings affect your actions.
It also helps you notice how others feel and respond in a kind and helpful way.

  • Self-awareness: Knowing how you feel and why.
    Example: You can tell when you’re feeling upset or sad, and you understand what’s causing it.
  • Self-regulation: Being able to control your emotions.
    Example: Instead of yelling when you’re angry, you take a deep breath and stay calm.
  • Motivation: Staying positive and working hard to reach your goals, even when things get difficult.
  • Empathy: Understanding how other people feel.
    Example: You notice when a friend is sad and respond in a supportive way.
  • People skills: Working well with others. This includes listening carefully, speaking clearly, solving problems, and building strong relationships.

How is EQ different to IQ?

EQ (Emotional Intelligence) and IQ (Intelligence Quotient) are both types of intelligence, but they focus on different things.

IQ is about:

  • Thinking skills
  • Solving problems
  • Learning and understanding facts
  • Memory and logic

IQ measures how well you do on tests, especially in areas like math, reading, and reasoning. People with high IQs are usually good at school subjects and academic tasks.

EQ is about:

  • Understanding emotions (your own and others)
  • Managing your feelings
  • Communicating clearly
  • Building strong relationships
  • Handling stress and conflict

People with high EQ are often good at working with others, staying calm under pressure, and leading or supporting a team.

Why is EQ important?

Emotional intelligence is important because it helps you to:

  • Handle stress better
  • Solve problems calmly
  • Communicate more clearly
  • Get along with people
  • Be a better friend, teammate, or leader

Other reasons why EQ matters:

  • EQ helps you stay calm, work well with others, and avoid arguments.
  • It helps you be a great team player.
  • You can improve your EQ with practice—just like learning any skill.
  • In everyday life, school, or work, EQ can sometimes be even more helpful than just being book-smart.
